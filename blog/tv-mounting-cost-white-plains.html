How Wall Type Affects TV Mounting Cost

The type of wall in your White Plains home plays a significant role in the setup required for mounting a TV:

  • Drywall over Wood Studs: This is the most common and standard installation. It is straightforward and efficient.
  • Plaster Walls: Many older homes in Westchester have lath and plaster walls. These require special anchors and extra care to prevent cracking.
  • Brick or Concrete: Mounting a TV on masonry requires a hammer drill and heavy-duty masonry anchors.
  • Metal Studs: Common in high-rise apartments and condos in White Plains and New Rochelle, metal studs require special toggle bolts, as standard wood screws will strip right out.

TV Size and Bracket Type

The size of your television also dictates the setup. A massive 85-inch TV often requires a two-person team to safely lift and secure the screen to the wall. Additionally, the type of bracket matters:

  • Fixed / Flush Mount: The simplest option. The TV sits flat against the wall.
  • Tilting Mount: Slightly more complex, allowing you to angle the screen down (perfect for mounting above a fireplace).
  • Full-Motion (Articulating) Mount: Allows you to pull the TV out and swivel it side-to-side. These brackets are heavy and require precise stud mounting to hold the torque of a fully extended TV.

Cable Hiding Options

A beautifully mounted TV can be ruined by a tangled mess of dangling black cords. Cable concealment is a popular add-on option.

External Cord Covers: Wires run down the face of the wall inside a paintable plastic track for a tidy appearance.

In-Wall Concealment: This premium look involves running the HDMI and power cables behind the drywall and bringing them out near the baseboard using code-compliant in-wall power kits.

When to Hire a Pro in White Plains

While some handy homeowners tackle TV mounting themselves, hiring a professional ensures your expensive television won't come crashing down. If you're dealing with plaster walls, brick, a TV over 65 inches, or you want wires hidden cleanly inside the wall, calling a local handyman is the safest bet for a perfect install.
