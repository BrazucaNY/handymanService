## 1. What I changed in the 39 answers

The original answers are strong, accurate, and careful. They are also 300-700 words each, which is too long for GBP surfaces. Homeowners on Google skim. Here is the adaptation logic:

| Original issue | GBP adaptation |
| --- | --- |
| Long multi-section answers | Cut to 600-900 character posts and 60-90 word Q&A answers |
| Repeats the same trust paragraph | One proof line per post, varied wording, to avoid duplicate content |
| Detailed disclaimers throughout | Keep the scope boundary in one short sentence, move detail to the website |
| No local signal in many answers | Add town names only where the business genuinely serves them |
| No next step | Every post ends with one CTA (Call, Book, or Message) |
| Price questions answered with caveats | Never publish a universal price; explain what changes the quote and offer a flat-rate quote from photos |
| Claims about insurance and credentials | Keep, but verify current documents and license numbers before publishing |
| Long lists | Trim to 3-7 bullets so the post reads on a phone |

The biggest strategic correction: **GBP Posts are not a primary ranking factor.** They support engagement, freshness, and conversion. The ranking levers that matter most are primary category, proximity, review quantity and quality, profile completeness, and website relevance. This plan prioritizes those first, then uses posts to convert the traffic you already earn.

### Accuracy checks before publishing

1. **Insurance and credentials.** The source says Here Handyman maintains business insurance and applicable Westchester County home-improvement credentials. Confirm the current documents and any license or registration number before repeating the claim on GBP. Be ready to send proof on request.
2. **Licensed trade boundaries.** Every electrical, plumbing, gas, structural, and permit-related answer already includes a referral line. Keep it. Do not let a post imply those tasks are included.
3. **Emergency framing.** Q19 is accurate but easy to misread. The posts make clear that a handyman is not emergency service. Keep that sentence.
4. **No price promises.** The source avoids universal prices. Keep it that way. Explain what changes a quote, not what a job "should" cost.
5. **Real service areas and photos.** Publish only towns you actually drive to and photos of work you actually completed. The listing must survive scrutiny.
6. **References.** Q33 says real project examples are available. Only say that if you can send them. Keep a folder of 10-15 before/after sets ready.

## 4. The posting system

### Cadence

- 2 posts per week, 24 posts over 12 weeks.
- Tuesday and Thursday, or Monday and Thursday, at a consistent time.
- One post should be service-intent (TV mounting, drywall) and one should be trust-intent (hiring, pricing, process).

### Post anatomy

1. Hook in the first 80-100 characters. That is all that shows before "more".
2. 3-7 short lines or bullets. No walls of text.
3. One proof line: owner-operated, insured, flat-rate quote, direct with David.
4. One CTA line that matches the button: "Tap Call", "Tap Book", "Tap Message".
5. Image: 1200x900 (4:3) or 1200x675 (16:9), JPG or PNG, real job photo.
6. CTA button: Call for urgent and trust posts, Book for service posts, Learn more when linking to a service page.
7. Link: booking or service page with UTM, for example `?utm_source=google&utm_medium=gbp&utm_campaign=post-w03-tv-mounting`.

### Rules that keep posts effective

- Do not paste a URL in the post text. It is not clickable. Use the CTA button.
- Do not use all caps or repeated phone numbers. Google can reject or suppress low-quality posts.
- Hashtags do little on GBP. Use zero to two, or none.
- Do not post the same text twice. Rotate topics and photos.
- Posts can expire or lose visibility after several months. Refresh the best performers every quarter.
- Offers require real terms and dates. Only publish a discount you will honor.
- Events need a real date range and location. Do not tag an event if it is just an ad.
- Keep the language about scope accurate. Never imply licensed trade work is included.

### 10-point pre-publish checklist

1. Hook readable on a phone in two lines.
2. One topic only.
3. Question number from the map noted.
4. One local signal, if natural.
5. No price promise.
6. Scope boundary stated where relevant.
7. One CTA.
8. Real photo, not stock.
9. Under 1,500 characters.
10. Link has UTM and opens the right page.

## 5. Q&A engine

GBP Q&A lets owners seed and answer common questions. Availability varies by region and account type, so if the Q&A module is not available, publish the same answers on your website FAQ and link to it from posts.

- Seed 10-13 questions over the first two weeks. Use the 13 in `GBP-PROFILE-COPY.md`.
- Upvote the best answer on each question.
- Answer new public questions within 24 hours, even if the answer is "send photos so I can confirm".
- Never argue publicly. Move disputes to a private call.
- Monitor weekly for spam or competitor answers.
- Refresh one Q&A per month with seasonal detail.

## 6. Review engine

Reviews are the strongest controllable trust signal and a real local ranking input.

### Ask

- Ask at the walkthrough, in person, when the customer is happiest.
- Send the direct review link by SMS or email the same day. One reminder after 3 days, then stop.
- Never offer a discount, gift, or entry for a review. Never gate customers by asking only happy ones.
- Goal: 4-8 new reviews per month, steady, not a burst.

### Respond

- Respond to every review within 48 hours.
- 2-4 sentences. Thank, name the service, add one local or process detail, invite them back. No personal information.
- For mixed or negative reviews: acknowledge, take responsibility for the experience, move it offline with a phone number, then follow up.
- Do not ask for the review to be removed. Flag only reviews that violate Google policy.

Templates are in `GBP-PROFILE-COPY.md`.

## 7. Local SEO around the profile

GBP does not rank alone. Pair it with:

1. **One page per core service** on the website, each mapped to a question cluster: TV mounting, drywall, doors, shelving, furniture assembly, caulking, trim, painting touch-ups.
2. **City pages only where you have real work and unique content.** Do not spin 20 thin pages. Two or three strong area pages beat twenty duplicates.
3. **Schema markup**: `HomeAndConstructionBusiness` or `LocalBusiness` with `areaServed`, `serviceType`, `sameAs`, and genuine review data only.
4. **NAP consistency** across the website, GBP, Yelp, Nextdoor, Angi, Thumbtack, BBB, and the local chamber.
5. **Internal links** from each post's CTA to the matching service page, and from service pages to the booking form.
6. **Photos with context**: real jobs in recognizable local home styles perform better than generic tool shots.
7. **Response speed**: a answered message or call within business hours is a conversion advantage no competitor listing can fake.

## 8. Measurement

Track weekly in GBP Performance:

- Calls
- Messages
- Website clicks
- Direction requests
- Bookings (if the booking link supports it)
- Search queries that triggered the profile

Use UTM-tagged links for every CTA so website analytics separates GBP traffic from organic search.

If you use a call-tracking number, keep the primary NAP number consistent and understand that dynamic number insertion can complicate citations. Simpler is better for a one-van business.

### 90-day benchmarks to adjust, not promises

| Metric | Day 30 | Day 60 | Day 90 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Profile completeness | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| Photos added | 10 | 25 | 40 |
| Q&A seeds live | 8 | 13 | 13 |

| New reviews | 4-6 | 10-14 | 18-24 |

| Website clicks from GBP | baseline | +15-25% | +30% |

Actual results depend on market density, proximity to searchers, competition, and review velocity. Review the query report every 30 days and add services or posts around the queries that already show up.

### Days 1-7: foundation

- Claim and verify the profile if not already done.
- Set name, primary category, secondary categories, service areas, hours, phone, website, booking link.
- Paste the description and all 14 services.
- Set truthful attributes and payment methods.
- Upload logo, cover, owner photo, van photo, 8-10 real job photos.
- Seed 8 Q&A entries.
- Request reviews from the last 5 happy customers.
- Add UTM to the website and booking links.

### Weeks 2-4: rhythm

- Publish posts 1-8.
- Add 3-5 photos per week.
- Answer every message and question within one business day.
- Respond to all reviews within 48 hours.
- Publish the first two service pages on the website.
- Audit NAP on the top 5 citations.

### Weeks 5-8: depth

- Publish posts 9-16.
- Add 5 more Q&A seeds.
- Publish two more service pages and one White Plains area page.
- Run one real seasonal offer post only if you have genuine availability or a real promotion.
- Add before/after photo sets for the four most requested services.
- Review GBP query report and note rising search terms.

### Weeks 9-12: compounding

- Publish posts 17-24.
- Refresh the three best-performing older posts with new photos and copy.
- Ask for reviews from every completed job.
- Add 10 more photos and one 60-second job walkthrough video if available.
- Compare day-1 and day-90 metrics.
- Double down on the two services that produced the most calls.
- Plan the next quarter around seasonal demand.

## 10. Compliance guardrails

- Do not add keywords to the business name. "Here Handyman" stays "Here Handyman".
- Do not create fake addresses, fake service areas, or duplicate listings.
- Do not buy, trade, or incentivize reviews. Do not gate review requests.
- Do not publish insurance, license, or certification claims until you have verified the current documents and any required license numbers.
- Do not promise universal prices. Quote from the actual scope.
- Do not imply that licensed plumbing, electrical, gas, structural, or other specialist work is included in handyman scope.
- Do not use stock photos of work you did not do.
- Do not publish a post that offers a discount you will not honor.
- Keep the editorial note from the source document: confirm each service is within applicable licensing and insurance requirements before publishing.
